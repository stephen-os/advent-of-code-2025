## My Thoughts

This was the first Advent of Code that I have completed in full. This is in part because it was shorter than normal and I chose to go with Python for my solutions, which I am a lot stronger with compared to Rust, which I attempted last year. I also want to give thanks to my professor Dr. Johnson for introducing me to Advent of Code back in college. This was a fun and rewarding side project to work on over the last couple of weeks.

I was happy and surprised to see how many problems and the types of problems I was able to solve on my own. I definitely came across problems that I could not solve and struggled with conceptually. I tried to notate solutions that I got from others or AI at the top of my solution file for reference. My one rule with this challenge was to actually try the problem and only seek help after attempting, no matter how hard. This left me with the mindset that I will try the problem with my toolbelt, but will go find and learn from a solution otherwise.

I think the solutions I did the best at were ones that involved linear arrays and intervals. I struggled more with graphs, though I was able to revisit depth-first search (DFS) techniques while working through those problems. I think I am 50/50 on 2D grid or multi-dimensional array problems. In Advent of Code compared to other types of puzzle problems, it was nice to find uses for some language features I don't use often enough such as `zip` and `enumerate`, all the ways to manipulate an array with `[x:x:x]` operations, and most importantly, Advent of Code will make you an expert at input parsing. I think input parsing may be one of the stronger skills I come away with and you can see my progress from day 1 all the way to day 12.

The time investment varied, but the hardest days took around an hour to an hour and a half. I took a break in the middle of the second week and got to the final three problems a week later. For most problems, I stuck to core Python with some basic math, and maybe one problem where I had to use `itertools`. 

One thing that really motivated me to solve these problems was doing this alongside my colleague Grayson. He solved his own puzzles and we got to compare our approaches on a lot of problems. It was interesting to see what we did that was similar and very interesting to see what we did that was different. Having that collaboration and discussion made the experience much more rewarding.

I hope to attempt this again next year in 2026 and I am kind of hoping that there are 24 problems this time to really put me to the test.

## Hardest Days

- **Day 12**: This day may have been the most difficult for me. There is a lot of complexity with the input parsing and if you are taking a backtracking approach, there are a lot of moving parts. I opted to cut the problem short and used a math solution that I found online. I give credit to this at the top of the solution.
- **Day 10**: This day I also used an online solution and still may need to further revisit this problem. I think what made this problem so difficult was the setup and finding the combinations.

## Favorite Days

- **Day 4**: I love 2D puzzles. It makes it feel much more like a game and you can more easily visualize the puzzle. This day was one of my favorites because I understood both parts and was able to come up with a solution with zero help.
- **Day 7**: This was another 2D puzzle and was themed around laboratories and lasers. I thought this puzzle was interesting because it combined traversing a grid in an interesting way with recursion. This is another one I was able to solve with minimal help.
